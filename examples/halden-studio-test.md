# An example test: making a starter site your own

Paste these into the create form. The blueprint is
`https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jamiemarsland/playground-usertest/main/examples/blueprint-halden-studio.json`,
which builds a plain Twenty Twenty-Five site called Halden Studio — three
pages, two posts, real photographs, and somebody else's name on all of it.

Nothing here tests a particular plugin or theme. It tests whether an ordinary
person can take a site somebody set up for them and make it theirs, which is
the thing most people are actually doing.

## What are you testing?

    the WordPress editor

## Who should they pretend to be?

> You are Priya Raman. For three years you have been making furniture in a
> rented workshop under the railway arches — chairs, mostly, and small tables,
> mostly for people who found you through a friend.
>
> You have just registered a business name, Raman & Daughter, and someone has
> set you up with a website to get started. It is not yours yet: it still has
> the name, the photographs and the words of the workshop it was copied from.
> You would like it to look like your work and sound like you, and you would
> like people to be able to reach you without having to ask around for your
> number.

The persona is deliberately not called Halden Studio. If the tester is already
playing the person whose name is on the site, the first task has nothing to
change.

## What should they try?

| Task | Hint |
|---|---|
| Put your own business name on the site, instead of the one it came with. | The name shows in the top-left of every page. Edit Site in the black bar opens the editor. |
| Change the big headline on the front page so it says what you make. | Click the words and type. Click somewhere else when you are done. |
| Swap one of the photographs for one of your own. | Click a photo and look for Replace, in the toolbar above it or the panel at the side. |
| Give the site a different look — colours, type, or both — and keep one you like. | Styles is the half-dark circle in the editor's top bar. |
| Write a couple of sentences about yourself on the About page. | You can reach it from the site's own menu, or from the list of pages in the editor. |
| Add a page called Prices, put something on it, and make sure a visitor could find it. | Making the page is one thing. Getting it into the menu at the top is another. |
| Look at the site the way a visitor would, and check how it looks on a phone. | There is a preview in the editor's top bar, and the site itself is a tab away. |

Seven is the top of what people will sit through. Five is safer if you are
sending it to someone who is doing you a favour.

## Why these seven

They are the things someone has to do before a site is theirs rather than a
template: the name, the first sentence a visitor reads, a picture, the look,
a page about them, a page that did not exist, and the check that it works for
everybody else. Each one can be done several ways, and none of them names a
button in the task itself — the hint is there for when they are stuck, and
whether they opened it is one of the more useful things the results tell you.

## Before you send it out

Walk it yourself once, then clear the store. Every dry run is a row in the
results, and it is hard to read ten real testers past your own five attempts.
